Pseudo-class and Pseudo-elements

Pseudo-classes and pseudo-elements are both used in CSS to style HTML elements, but they have different purposes. A pseudo-class is used to select an element based on its state or condition, such as when the mouse is over it, when it is focused, or when an input is invalid. Examples of pseudo-classes are :hover, :focus, and :invalid. On the other hand, a pseudo-element is used to style or create a specific part of an element, such as the first letter, the first line, or content before or after an element. Examples of pseudo-elements are ::before, ::after, and ::first-letter. In short, pseudo-classes describe the state of an element, while pseudo-elements style a specific part of an element.
